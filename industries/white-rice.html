White Rice Processing Plant & Machinery Solutions

Turnkey plant · Machinery · Automation · India · UAE · Thailand

White rice processing removes the husk and the outer bran layer from paddy to produce polished, uniform grain for table and export use. Cleaned paddy is first dehusked, the dehusked grain is then whitened and polished, and the milled rice is graded and colour sorted before packing.

How it works

Complete White Rice Process Flow

Paddy Intake & Pre-Cleaning

Incoming paddy is fed from the intake point and screened to remove straw, chaff, mud balls, stones and stray ferrous particles. Clean paddy protects the huller rolls and keeps foreign matter out of the finished rice.

Pre-CleanerAir Screen CleanerDe-StonerMagnetic SeparatorBucket Elevator
Dehusking & Husk Separation

The husk is split off the grain and drawn away by aspiration, leaving brown rice mixed with a share of paddy that has not yet shelled. That mixture is separated so unhusked grain returns to the huller and only dehusked rice moves forward.

DehuskingAir AspiratorWinnowerGravity SeparatorCyclone Separator
Whitening

The bran layer and germ are abraded off the brown rice, which is what turns the grain white. Whitening is normally taken in stages rather than a single pass, and the bran removed is drawn off the machine for separate handling.

Milling MachineDust Extraction SystemCyclone Dust Collector MachineScrew Conveyor
Polishing

Polishing finishes the whitened grain, lifting residual bran dust off the surface and giving the smooth appearance expected of table rice. Polished rice is cooled and settled before it goes on to grading.

PolisherCooling ConveyorAir Aspirator
Grading & Colour Sorting

Whole grains, brokens and small brokens are separated by size, and the graded rice then passes an optical sorter that rejects discoloured, chalky and damaged grains. Grading fixes the commercial grade of each stream, so head rice and brokens can be handled and sold separately.

GraderScreenerColor Sorter
Weighing, Inspection & Packing

Graded rice is held in silos, blended where a particular grade mix is required, then weighed and filled into consumer packs or bulk sacks. Filled packs are checked and pass metal detection before they are stitched or sealed and made ready for despatch.
